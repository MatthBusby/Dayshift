Downtown Calgary has sixteen kilometres of indoor walkway. Nobody has a map worth using.

Dayshift is a free directory, live-status layer and wayfinding tool for the Plus 15, downtown Calgary's skywalk network. Closed beta this winter, public in January 2027.

The largest skywalk system in the world, and it is genuinely hard to use

The Plus 15 opened in 1970, fifteen feet above the street, and has been growing ever since. It is run by the City of Calgary together with the private owners of the buildings it passes through, which is why the coverage is dense in the commercial core and ragged at the edges.

Tens of thousands of people move through it every weekday. Most of them know the ten or fifteen places within a two-bridge radius of their own desk and nothing at all beyond that. Shops open and close constantly. The official maps are PDFs.

Hybrid work has made this worse rather than better. Someone in the office five days a week built a mental map inside a month. Someone in twice a week never quite does, and is forever being summoned to a meeting in a tower they have never entered.

Three problems, and only one of them is a map problem

Knowing what's there

There is no browsable list of what's inside the network. Good places two bridges away go unvisited for years because nobody has any way to find out about them.

Knowing what's open

Most food counters in the Plus 15 shut between 2:30 and 3:00. At 1:50 nothing on earth tells you who is still serving, and that is a real moment of panic with no current answer.

Getting there

Most people don't need turn-by-turn directions. They need to know a place exists and roughly how to reach it without stepping outside in February.

What it is, and what it isn't

It is
  • A browsable directory of everything inside the network
  • A live status layer: open now, closing soon, closed today
  • Building-to-building directions written the way a person would say them out loud
  • A quick way to agree on where to meet
  • Free for anyone who walks the network, permanently
It isn't
  • An indoor floorplan or turn-by-turn navigation system
  • A blue dot tracking you through a building
  • A replacement for the signage on the walls
  • A coupon or discount aggregator
  • A social network
  • A food delivery app

What's in it

Directory and map

Every shop, café and counter in the network with its category, building, level, hours, payment types and a photo of the storefront. Results are ranked by how long it actually takes to walk there, not by how close it looks on a map.

Open right now

A live open indicator, a warning when a place is inside its last hour, and a view at ten to two that shows only what is still serving. If a counter shuts early, one tap reports it for everyone else.

Getting there

Pick your building from a short list, get a clean schematic line to your destination and a written description of each bridge crossing. There is a step-free route option for anyone who needs the elevators.

The things nobody maps

Washrooms, ATMs, mail drops, bike storage, transit connections, and quiet corners where you can take a call. Useful on your first day and still useful on your thousandth.

Help deciding

Three suggestions a day, weighted away from wherever you have been lately. On a minus twenty-eight morning, one tap shows everything you can reach without going outside.

Meeting people

Drop a pin and text the link. It opens straight to a map and a route, with nothing to install and nothing to sign into. For a group, put two to four options up and let everyone tap.

Three ways to be part of this

Dayshift is two people building something for the city they walk through every day. All three of these help, and none of them cost anything.

Shops and cafés

A basic listing is free and stays free. You keep your hours right, we send people who had no idea you existed. Later you'll be able to put whatever's left at the end of the day in front of people at quarter to two, when the counter has gone quiet and it would otherwise be thrown out.

Email us about listing

Building owners and managers

We're not selling you anything. Your amenities, your events and your retail vacancies are invisible from one bridge away, because nothing crosses building boundaries. Send us what's happening in your towers and we'll put it on the map for the people already walking through them.

Email us about your building

Testers

We need about fifty people who walk the Plus 15 most days and are willing to tell us when it's wrong. You'll get early builds well before the public listing, your name on the contributors page if you want it, and a direct line to the two people building it.

Where we are

Autumn 2026

Walking the core on foot. Every shop, every bridge, every washroom, recorded by hand. No code gets written until this is done properly.

Winter 2026

Closed beta with about fifty testers. Directory first, then the live open-now layer and early-closure reporting.

January 2027

Public launch, timed for the part of the year when the Plus 15 sells itself. Routing, written bridge directions, the utility layer and the passport.

Through 2027

Tools for merchants: claim your listing, run your own hours, and post what's left at the end of the day.
